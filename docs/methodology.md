# Methodology

## Question

Fraud is rare: 3.5% of the transactions in the IEEE-CIS dataset. Does adding **synthetic fraud
generated by a GAN, guided by an autoencoder's anomaly score**, help a gradient-boosted
classifier rank fraud better than training on the real data alone, or than oversampling with
SMOTE?

## Data and split

- **Source:** the Kaggle IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection training files. `train_transaction.csv` and
  `train_identity.csv` are left-joined on `TransactionID` (not every transaction has an
  identity record): 590,540 transactions, 434 columns.
- **Split:** temporal, by `TransactionDT`. The earliest 70% is train, the next 20% validation,
  the latest 10% test. Nothing is shuffled, so every model is scored on transactions that
  happened after the ones it learned from, as in deployment.

| Split | Rows before cleaning | Rows after cleaning |
|---|---|---|
| train | 413,378 | 413,165 |
| validation | 118,108 | 118,107 |
| test | 59,054 | 59,053 |

Rows are removed for excessive missing values (train only) and for exact duplicates. After
cleaning there are 237 columns: 236 predictors and the `isFraud` label. The training split has
14,520 frauds (3.51%).

## Cleaning

Every statistic is learned on the training split only and replayed on validation and test, so
no later split influences the preprocessing. In order:

1. Drop columns with more than 55% missing values (measured on train).
2. Drop training rows with more than 48% missing values (validation and test keep all rows).
3. Drop the transaction id.
4. Fill missing values: median for quantitative columns, mode for two-valued columns, a
   sentinel for numeric codes, `"unknown"` for the other categoricals. Drop duplicates.
5. Encode: `T`/`F` to 1/0; one-hot for columns with fewer than 10 categories; label encoding
   otherwise.
6. Replace `TransactionDT` by day, hour and minute.
7. Shift and log-transform the quantitative columns with absolute skew above 0.5.
8. Standardise every non-binary column.

### Known quirks

Both come from the original pipeline and are kept so that the data, and therefore every result,
stays comparable with the work the thesis reports.

- **Train row order.** Selecting rows by missing-value count leaves the training rows ordered
  from most to least missing. The autoencoder trains without shuffling, so its batches follow
  this order.
- **`P_emaildomain` is constant.** The label encoding of this column maps every value to 0,
  so one of the 236 predictors carries no information. It affects every method equally.

## Models

**Classifier.** LightGBM with library defaults for every method, so differences between
methods come from the training data and not from tuning.

**Autoencoder.** A multilayer perceptron (input, 128, 16, 128, input) with dropout 0.3 in the
encoder, trained with mean squared error and Adam (learning rate 1e-3) for 30 epochs on
legitimate training transactions only. Fraud is therefore reconstructed worse, and the
reconstruction error is an anomaly signal. The **anomaly score** is `log(1 + error)`,
standardised with the training errors.

**GAN.** A Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty and a spectrally normalised critic. The
generator maps 128-dimensional noise through layers of 512, 512, 512 and 256 units (batch
normalisation, LeakyReLU) to a transaction; the critic has layers of 256 and 128 units.
Adam (learning rate 1e-4, betas 0.5 and 0.9), one critic step per generator step, batch size
256, 1000 epochs. It is trained on the real fraud rows of the training split only.

**Conditional GAN (cGAN).** The same GAN with a one-dimensional condition, the autoencoder
anomaly score, concatenated to the generator's noise and to the critic's input. During training
each real fraud is shown together with its own score. To generate, scores are drawn with
replacement from the real frauds' scores, keeping their share in each of three equal-width
score bins, so the generated frauds follow the anomaly profile of real fraud. Gradient penalty
weight 2 and learning rates halved at epoch 600.

**SMOTE.** The imbalanced-learn implementation with default neighbours, applied to the same
training data.

## Oversampling ratios

Each method adds fraud until a target is reached, and the two kinds of method define the
target differently:

- **SMOTE:** the ratio is `fraud / legitimate` after oversampling.
- **GAN and cGAN:** the ratio is the fraud count as a share of the *original* row count.

For the same number the GAN target is larger by a constant factor, original rows divided by
legitimate rows (about 1.036), which means more synthetic rows:

| Ratio | SMOTE: fraud (added) | GAN: fraud (added) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 19,932 (5,412) | 20,658 (6,138) |
| 0.10 | 39,864 (25,344) | 41,316 (26,797) |
| 0.50 | 199,322 (184,802) | 206,582 (192,062) |

Each method's ratio is chosen independently, as the one with the best validation PR-AUC in its
development experiments, so the two definitions are never compared head to head.

## Evaluation protocol

1. **Development.** Every experiment in `configs/experiments/development/` is scored on the
   validation split over a sweep of ratios (see [experiments](experiments.md)).
2. **Validation stage.** For each of baseline, SMOTE, GAN and AE-cGAN: fit on the training split
   plus the method's synthetic fraud, score on validation, and measure how close the synthetic
   samples are to real fraud.
3. **Test stage.** Refit every method on **training + validation** plus synthetic fraud and score
   the held-out test split. The generators are **not** retrained for this stage: they stay as
   trained on the training split. Only the classifier, the SMOTE fit, the number of synthetic
   rows and the pool of anomaly scores use training + validation. The test split is only ever
   scored, never used to fit or to choose anything.

## Metrics

- **PR-AUC** (average precision) is the primary metric. With 3.5% fraud, accuracy and ROC-AUC
  look good even for weak models.
- **ROC-AUC**, and **precision at 80, 85, 90, 95 and 100% recall.** Precision at a recall level
  is read from the precision-recall curve at the point whose recall is closest to that level;
  when several thresholds give the same recall the first (lowest-threshold, lowest-precision)
  point is used. At 100% recall this is the point where everything is flagged, so the value is
  just the fraud prevalence (0.033 on validation, 0.037 on test) for every method. It is kept
  in the result files and carries no information.
- **False alerts saved:** for each recall from 5% to 100%, the number of legitimate transactions
  flagged by the baseline minus the number flagged by the method, at the threshold that
  catches that share of the fraud.
- **Synthetic quality** (validation stage): mean 1-D Wasserstein distance between real and
  synthetic features, mean absolute difference of the correlation matrices, and nearest-neighbour
  distances in both directions.

## Reproducibility

- Every run takes its seed from the config (11 for all reported results). Global seeds are set
  and PyTorch deterministic algorithms are requested.
- Every experiment is a config; results (tables, histories, figures) are committed under
  `results/`, and each result folder contains a copy of the config that produced it.
  Checkpoints and generated samples go to `artifacts/` and are not committed.
- The data pipeline, the autoencoder, the baseline and SMOTE reproduce exactly. GAN and cGAN
  training is deterministic on one machine and software stack but **not bit-identical across
  machines**, so a retrained generator gives numbers close to, not equal to, an earlier run.
  The training code was checked against the original implementation: on CPU, with the same
  seed, all iterations produced identical weights.

## Differences from the thesis

The thesis results were produced by the original notebooks. This repository reproduces the data
pipeline and the baseline exactly (validation PR-AUC 0.5076, test 0.4894), but four things differ
and the final numbers change as a result:

| | Thesis | This repository |
|---|---|---|
| SMOTE ratio | 0.075, described as the best on validation | chosen by the stated rule: 0.05 (validation PR-AUC 0.5178 against 0.5143) |
| cGAN training | each fraud was shown with the anomaly score of an unrelated row (the score table covers all training rows while the fraud rows are a subset, and both were indexed by position) | each fraud is shown with its own score |
| GAN and cGAN | trained once on the original hardware | retrained; not bit-identical |
| Test PR-AUC (baseline / SMOTE / GAN / AE-cGAN) | 0.4894 / 0.4948 / 0.5319 / 0.5353 | 0.4894 / 0.5370 / 0.5315 / 0.5418 |

With SMOTE at the ratio the rule selects, it is no longer clearly behind the generative models.

## Limitations

- **One seed.** The differences between the three oversampling methods (about 0.01 test PR-AUC)
  are comparable to the spread between near-identical development configurations (0.510 to
  0.522 validation PR-AUC), so no ranking among them is claimed. Several seeds with confidence
  intervals are the natural next step.
- **One dataset.**
- **The two ratio definitions differ** (above); a like-for-like comparison would harmonise them.
- **The two cleaning quirks** (above) are inherited.
- **The gain over the baseline is much smaller on validation (+0.010) than on test (+0.042 to
  +0.052),** which shows how noisy a single split and seed are.
